How To Protect Your Drainfield

Simple property and water-use habits can help protect the drainfield and support healthy septic operation.

DRAINFIELD CARE

Protect Your Drainfield For Long-Term Septic Performance

The drainfield is an important part of a septic system. It allows treated wastewater to move into the surrounding soil for further natural treatment. Proper care can help protect the drainfield from unnecessary stress and damage.

1. Keep Heavy Vehicles Away

Avoid driving or parking cars, trucks, or heavy equipment over the drainfield. Excessive weight can compact the soil and potentially damage underground pipes and distribution components.

2. Avoid Building Over The Drainfield

Sheds, patios, decks, pools, and other permanent structures should not be placed over the drainfield. Keeping the area accessible also makes future inspection and maintenance easier.

3. Manage Water Usage

Excessive water entering the septic system can place additional hydraulic stress on the drainfield. Spread laundry and other high-water activities throughout the week when possible.

4. Keep Surface Water Away

Rainwater from roofs, gutters, driveways, and other surfaces should be directed away from the drainfield. Additional surface water can saturate the soil and reduce its ability to absorb wastewater.

5. Choose Landscaping Carefully

Avoid planting trees and large shrubs directly over the drainfield. Their roots can interfere with underground pipes. Grass and shallow-rooted plants are generally easier to manage in this area.

6. Watch For Changes Around The Area

Pay attention to unusually wet soil, standing water, sewage odors, unusually green grass, or slow drainage near the drainfield. These changes may indicate that the system needs professional evaluation.

Drainfield Reminder

Keep the drainfield clear of heavy structures, vehicles, excessive surface water, and inappropriate landscaping to help support proper septic system operation.
